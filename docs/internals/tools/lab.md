# `tools/lab.py`, `measure/lab/` — the lab: small CUDA experiments that become kernel rules

The kernels are written to rules about the hardware and the compiler — what a pipe costs beside the tensor pipe, what
ptxas does to a pattern, when two warps interfere. Those rules come from measurements, and a measurement taken in a
one-off probe is easy to get wrong and impossible to repeat. The lab makes an experiment cheap to write and safe to
trust, and keys every record by the architecture, so a rule says where it was measured and a new card or toolkit
re-runs the experiments its rules cite (`docs/KERNEL_STANDARDS.md` §26).

## The loop

1. A QUESTION about the machine, asked as one experiment, `measure/lab/experiments/<name>.cu`: its header states the
   QUESTION and the METHOD, its kernels are template instantiations (one per variant), and its `main` declares the SASS
   each kernel must contain and times the variants.
2. `bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run` builds the experiment's target ([below](#graph): the graph compiles it for sm_90a, its
   disassemblers read it and its checks are the build's validation), hands the lab that build, reads the SASS and
   REFUSES to measure unless every declared expectation holds, every notice the toolkit printed is allowed by [the ledger](#ledger) (a kernel that spills or keeps a stack frame is the toolkit's own warning) and no kernel holds a
   convergence call nobody declared (the checker's `convergence_call`, as the SASS gate reads it: ptxas's loop-exit
   `CALL.REL.NOINC` and guarded fallbacks are not ones); checks the GPU is quiet; runs the repetitions; prints median, min and max and flags a result
   whose samples spread past 5%.
3. It STORES the record through the results store (`fsla_devtools.results`) at its run target's label, in the store's checkout (the
   dev config's `store.root`, the data repository, never this tree): a sample of the record its question keys
   (`measure/data.py`'s `lab_semantics`: the experiment, the architecture, the kind `run`, the grid `--blocks` held it to),
   its output the run's body — the device, driver, toolkit, commit, the results with their samples, and each kernel's
   registers and leading opcodes — and, for an experiment with traced rows, its PARTS `trace/row<i>.json`, a warpline record
   a traced row, each checked by warpline ([`#trace`](#trace)); its provenance the workspace's stamp (the run target's
   `:stamp`), the binary's digest, the toolkit and the machine. A new run is a new sample, never an overwrite; a reader
   takes the newest (`lab_record`). Cited once a pushed commit of the data is the pin, as the sample's output,
   `data:records/fsla/measure/lab/<name>.run/<run>/output.json` ([`measurement.md`](../../measurement.md#the-data)); a rule cites it.
   `-- run --no-store` keeps the record in a store under the scratch space instead, which `trace` and `path` read with
   `--data`.
4. The finding becomes a law in `docs/KERNEL_STANDARDS.md` (§26), naming the experiment, the record and the
   architectures it holds on.
5. Write `docs/lab/<name>.md` and its row in `docs/lab/README.md`; its measured section is generated by
   `lab -- page <name> --instance LABEL`, never typed.

THE TWO ENTRIES. Every command that reads an experiment's build runs from the experiment's run target,
`bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- <command> ...` (`run` when none is given; `sass`, `ncu`, `ledger`, `waits`, `steps`,
`pack`), which hands `tools/lab.py` the build in its runfiles and the stamp -- no command builds anything; the others read
records or a pack, from `bazel run //tools:lab -- <command> <name> ...` (`list`, `path`, `trace`, `page`, `carried`,
`ledger-dumps`, `ledger --packed`), and each entry refuses the other's commands by name.
`bazel run //tools:lab -- list` names the experiments by their QUESTION;
`bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- sass [REGEX] [--full]` shows each kernel's registers and leading opcodes (or the whole
disassembly). `bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- ncu --metric M [--metric ...]` reads
hardware counters per kernel (the median over its launches), stored as a record of its own (the kind `counters`), for a
question about traffic rather than time.
`bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- ledger [--kernel REGEX]` attributes a kernel's executed instructions to its phases --
the `//:` headings of the experiment's source -- per declared unit (KERNEL_STANDARDS §29.13; the method:
[budgets.md](budgets.md)). `run` also refuses a budgeted kernel whose loops hold convergence instructions (`BSSY`,
`BSYNC`, `BRA.DIV`) beyond what it declares as `LOOP_CONVERGENCE`. `-- ledger --static --arch sm_90a` needs no device and no counters (without `--arch` it reads the
device's): it prints each
kernel's instructions AS COMPILED, by the role that holds them (the `NAME_role` call they were inlined from), by
innermost natural loop (the checker's model of the kernel; an instruction in none is outside every loop) and by phase and kind (global loads, stores and adds, shared
loads and stores, MMAs); a loop's cost a unit is its count times its trips, which the source states. It is the
ledger of a card that grants no profiler counters.
`bazel run //tools:lab -- path <name> [--row REGEX] [--blocks N] [--variant V] [--data DIR]` names THE WINDOW'S PATH from a record's
clocked rows (KERNEL_STANDARDS §22): for each row whose sites name its roles' warps (`<row>: a <role> warp ...`, in
cycles a window a warp), each role's waits (its sites that `wait on` something, its rendezvous and fences; a
warpgroup's first warp's left out),
its busy cycles (the window less them) and its phases, a phase that holds a wait marked; the role that waits least
is the path, and only its phases can shorten the window -- unless the two least-waiting roles are within 5% of the
window of each other, when the row names no path. Read it before any role's stall samples: a role with slack stalls too.
Its limit: it reads sums, and a cycle no site clocks counts as busy -- the serve rows' drain warps read 82% busy with
their copies' waits unclocked (2026-11-15). A TRACED row's timeline is read by `trace` instead.
<a id="trace"></a>`bazel run //tools:lab -- trace <name> [--row REGEX] [--blocks N] [--variant V] [--data DIR] [--skip N] [--html FILE]` reads a record's TRACED
rows (`tools/lab_trace.py`): every warp's stamps (`lab::Trace`) -- each phase's start in a coarse row, and in a fine one the start and end of each
wait, each arrival and each copy's issue too -- stored as the sample's parts, WARPLINE'S NATIVE RECORDS, `trace/` of
`row<i>.json`, a traced row each, in the order the rows ran (the record's `variant` its name), two CTAs a row (the first and
the middle one; a one-CTA grid's, its one: a scope `cta<N>` each, a warp a lane `warp<W>`), the timed launches' last. The
directory is the container because each of warpline's commands (`python -m warpline check|path|window|rates|perfetto
<record>`) takes one record by its path, so a row's file is what they are pointed at; a variant's name is far longer than a
file name allows, so it is in the record. `Bench::trace` writes each record (`trace_record`, host code in `lab.cuh`, through
warpline's `host.hpp`: [below](#warpline)) and prints one line, `{"lab": "trace", "variant", "record"}`, which `tools/lab.py` gathers into the
directory after warpline's checks on each (a record with any finding is not recorded, with no exemption: a row with no launch
exit -- a fine row -- is read by warpline's one rule, its open regions closed at its last stamp and counted as `open_at_end`, which
is no finding). A record's variant is its field `variant` (`tools/lab_trace.py`'s `variant_of`); a record the lab wrote before the pin
carried the same string as `source.variant`, which is read where `variant` is absent, so the committed records stay as they were
written. THE LAUNCH'S EDGES: a coarse row's warps and an EDGES row's
(the plain kernel with its edges and nothing else stamped) each mark an `entry`, at the kernel's top before its setup, and an `exit`, at its
role's end, on both clocks -- the record's `launch.begin` and `launch.end`; a fine row holds none (its kernels are at their registers' limit). A role's tracer writes after the
entry's mark (`bind(rows, after)`: `after` the entries already written to the warp's row), so no tracer's state crosses the
roles' register calls. Where a row's warps carry the edges `trace` prints each CTA's LAUNCH line (warpline's `launch.launch_of`): the first warp's entry to the
last warp's exit in cycles and in nanoseconds, the rate between them (cycles a microsecond), and the warps' spread at the
entry and at the exit; an edges row, which holds no window, prints that and its untraced twin's window beside its own
(the twin is the row's name with its trace's suffix removed: `, the launch's two edges stamped`, `, every warp's phases
stamped` or `, every warp's timeline stamped`). The edges are outside the steady span below: it is bounded by the windows' starts, so
the setup before the first window and the tail after the last are in no table. Over the steady span (warpline's `reader.steady` on the `window` phase: past the first `--skip` windows, to the last window
every warp's stamps cover) it prints each role's phases in the role's own window -- start, duration and rate in cycles a microsecond (warpline's
`window.role_windows` and `rates.analyze`) -- and then what warpline has no notion of, read from the record's stamps: each role's cycles a window a warp by state (a phase's work, a wait at a barrier,
a wait on its own steps); each BLOCKING wait (over 64 cycles) with its releaser -- the latest arrival at its barrier
at or before its end (a named barrier's last warp to reach it; a copy's barrier, the copy's issue) -- and what the
releaser was doing; and THE PATH, walked back from the span's end: a warp's own time to its latest blocking wait, then
the releaser's from its release, to the span's start, so every cycle of the span is one warp's state, a copy's flight
or a release's latency, each release printed with the mean MARGIN it binds by (its waits' length: a release binding
by a few hundred cycles has the waiter's own chain nearly as long beside it, and shortening the release alone gains no
more than that margin). `--html` draws three windows of each row's first CTA, a lane a warp, a blocking wait's release
a line from its releaser. The traced row's window beside its untraced twin's is the trace's own cost.
`bazel run //tools:lab -- page <name> --instance LABEL` regenerates an experiment's page's measured section from its
record: it imports `measure/lab/pages/<name>.py` and calls its `write(root, data, instance)`, which reads, in the pinned data,
the newest sample of its record (`measure/lab/pages/_record.py`'s `read`, `lab_record`) and rewrites the docs that quote it, and prints the files written; an
experiment with no page module is an error. A page's measured section is GENERATED, never typed: a figure changes by
a new record and a `page` run, and `LABEL` (the rented box's id; two comma-joined where a page's two records ran on two boxes) only words the card's line. OWED: only the carry's, the gather rate's, the gather group's,
the token carry's, the gather's, the stamp probe's and the clock probe's pages have their modules; every other page's typed section stands as recorded and is not edited by
hand -- its module is written with its experiment's next record. A row a page names and its record lacks is refused
by name: the generators' one lookup, `measure/lab/pages/_record.py`.
`ledger <name> --packed <binary> --dump <csv> [--dump ...] --arch sm_90a --per <n>` reads a CARRIED ledger: the
SourceCounters dumps a rented box made of `lab pack`'s binary (`ncu --section SourceCounters --page source
--print-source sass --csv`, a kernel's launch after the first `--skip` of them, one file a kernel named
`ledger_<its template numbers>.csv`, and `ledger.json` naming the binary's digest, the launch and each kernel's file),
mapped here to the phases through that same binary; `--per` divides every count by the launch's CTA-windows. Each
phase reports its executed instructions, its shared-memory wavefronts, those over the conflict-free count (bank
conflicts), its share of the stall samples and, for the phases holding the most, their three largest stall reasons;
then the source lines issuing the shared wavefronts. `ledger-dumps --skip N` profiles each kernel's launch after its
first N: an experiment's first is its checked launch, the timed ones follow.
<a id="profile-checked"></a>A record's PROFILE IS CHECKED (KERNEL_STANDARDS 30): `ledger` runs the checker's profile rule `shared_wavefronts_excess` on each kernel's dump (a carried one or a live one)
and REFUSES, exit 1, where a kernel's shared wavefronts in excess of the conflict-free count are over what the experiment declares -- zero undeclared. The refusal names the
kernel, then each source line that issues the excess with its count a CTA-window (`--per`) and in the launch, the checker's fix and fsla's note. Declare it as
`b.expect(k, "BANK_CONFLICT", "<=", n)` with its `//:` reason above it (the [hazards](#hazards)): `n` is the excess wavefronts of a CTA-WINDOW
of the profiled launch (the dump's count over the launch's CTA-windows, `--per`), never of the launch -- a launch's total moves with the grid and the
windows a row times, so a declaration of it is no bound. The refusal prints both, the excess and the declaration a CTA-window and in the launch; a profile whose
CTA-windows are not stated (no `--per`) reads no declaration. A conflict a row measures on purpose (a probe of the conflict) states its count there. A row is not read as a result before this holds.

<a id="hazards"></a>THE LEARNINGS' CHECKS ([`LEARNINGS.md`](../LEARNINGS.md)). The hazards are WARPLINE'S CHECKER'S RULES (`warpline.checker`, the module
`MODULE.bazel` pins and `bazel run //tools:lab` hands in): the lab computes none of them. A refusal prints the finding, the checker's own fix and then fsla's `note:` for the rule, the `guidance` of the rule's entry in the `checker` section of `docs/rules.json` (`guidance()` in `tools/lab.py`; [`lint.md`](lint.md#rules-table)). `check` translates the experiment's
`Bench::expect(kernel, NAME, "<=", n)` declarations to the checker's (kernel regex, rule, allowed count, reason -- the reason is the `//:` comment
block above the `expect` where exactly one stands above the experiment's declarations of that NAME, else a fixed text saying it is in the source),
reads the kernels into the checker's model (named as the toolkit's `cu++filt` names them: the patterns `lab::kernel` writes are of that form),
runs the rules and shows each refusal under the hazard's name the experiment declares. Each hazard is zero unless declared:

| hazard (`HAZARDS`) | the checker's rule | what it counts |
|---|---|---|
| `LOOP_CONVERGENCE` | `loop_convergence` | `BSSY`, `BSYNC`, `BRA.DIV` inside a natural loop |
| `LOAD_IN_TRIP` | `load_in_trip` | global loads whose value an instruction of the same innermost trip reads |
| `STORE_RELOAD` | `store_reload` | shared loads of an address the same trip stored to |
| `STORE_UNFENCED` | `store_unfenced` | in a kernel that holds a wgmma, barrier arrivals after a shared store with no async-proxy fence between |
| `CLOCK_UNIFORM` | `clock_uniform` | a cycle-counter reading summed in uniform registers |
| `WAIT_AFTER_ISSUE` | `wait_after_issue` | a tensor wait that leaves no group in flight directly after its group's steps (KERNEL_STANDARDS 29.16) |
| `STEP_ROLLED` | `step_rolled` | tensor steps in the innermost natural loop that holds no wait on them (the rule counts the steps; the declaration is a count of them) |
| `STEP_SERIALIZED` | `step_serialized` | a run of four tensor steps or more in one innermost loop in which at most one step in eight lacks its own `DEPBAR` 0 before the next (the count is the runs) |
| `ACCUMULATOR_MOVED` | `accumulator_moved` | an instruction that is no step, writes a register of a step's destination and reads one, between two steps of a chain or a step and its wait (a copy or an update of an accumulator) |
| `WAIT_ALL_WITH_WORK` | `wait_all_with_work` | a `DEPBAR` 0 after which nothing up to the next step touches the newest group's registers while work of substance is on its path: a wait for fewer groups would let it overlap |
| `STEP_PORT`, `STEP_WORK` | `step_port`, `step_work` | a BOUND, declared `<=`: the bytes a step puts through the shared-memory port, the instructions a thread issues a step |
| `STEPS_SERIALIZED`, `WAIT_INJECTED`, `REGISTER_SPILL`, `LOCAL_MEMORY_USED` | `mma_serialized`, `wait_injected`, `register_spill`, `local_memory_used` | the toolkit's notices ([below](#ledger)): the count a kernel may have, declared with its reason (the spill and the stack frame an experiment's compile leaves in a kernel it means) |
| `BANK_CONFLICT` | `shared_wavefronts_excess` | a PROFILE rule, run by `ledger` on the dumps, not by `check`: the shared-memory wavefronts over the conflict-free count, by instruction, declared a CTA-window of the profiled launch (`--per`), never a launch's total |
| `BSSY`, `STEP_XU`, `STEP_GLOBAL`, a `CALL` opcode | `step_branches`, `step_conversion`, `step_global`, `convergence_call` | always checked in every kernel; an expectation of one of these names is a PERMIT (any number in the kernels it matches), not a count |

A declaration of `BSSY`, `STEP_XU`, `STEP_GLOBAL` or a `CALL` opcode is a presence permit and its number is never compared: `<= 40` permits them in the kernels it names and does not bound them (`STEP_XU` is no opcode).

A loop is a natural loop of the kernel's graph (a back edge to a block that dominates its source), the blocks it holds wherever the
toolkit laid them out: an address span between a backward branch and its target is no loop of it (a `try_wait`'s out-of-line retry, a stub's return jump
and a cold block laid inside such a span are not in one). The static ledger reads the same loops. What stays in `tools/lab.py` is the project's own: a bound
declared `<=` and naming a kernel with tensor steps (`bound_failures`), the DEBTS below, the refusals' texts (`REFUSAL_TEXT`), and the checks of
the experiment's own source -- an experiment outside `_PRE_TIMELINE` (the experiments written before these checks; the list only shrinks) is
refused by `run` and by `pack` unless its header comment states a `TIMELINE:` -- each phase's stream, which load is a stage ahead of which use,
the live registers -- and where its source has a warp collective after a conditional's `?` (`collective_selected`: only the lanes the condition
takes reach a collective every lane must). Past `_PRE_TIMELINE` every kernel of an experiment, budgeted or exempt, is held to every hazard;
inside it, `LOOP_CONVERGENCE` is held in budgeted kernels only and the others not at all. The experiments past `_PRE_TIMELINE` that had
`WAIT_AFTER_ISSUE` when the check landed are `_PRE_WAIT` (held to it when next changed; the list only shrinks). An experiment that keeps a hazard on
purpose declares its count in `main` with its reason (`carry_window`'s ready skipping rows their C7520, `fold_forms`'s tile forms and
`carry_window`'s packed rows their steps in a loop, `wgmma_regs`'s narrowed forms their injected wait, `carry_token` and `carry_window` their waits
straight after the group's steps). The advisories `lab waits` (what could precede a wait, `waits_report`) and `lab steps` (the figures of
`step_port` and `step_work` over each region of a kernel) never refuse.

<a id="ledger"></a>THE LEDGER OF THE TOOLKIT'S NOTICES (`tools/notice_ledger.json`), the project's default for every build, the lab's and the production gate's
(the SASS gate's check reads the units' ptxas reports against it too, `--ledger`). nvcc and ptxas say, beside the code, what they did to
it: a loop they proved unreachable, a kernel's asynchronous MMAs serialized, a register they spilled. The checker reads EVERY
notice and warning of a build (`notices.read_notices`: ptxas's `info` and `warning` lines with a code and without, nvcc's
`file(line): warning #N-D: ...`, a bare `Potential Performance Loss: ...`) and looks each up in the ledger -- its BASE ledger with the project's, `tools/notice_ledger.json`: a project row overrides the base row of the same tool, code and text or adds one, and the file's seven rows are the ones whose reason and fix name the lab's pages and constructs. One row
for each notice either tool can print that the lab has seen or knows the template of -- the toolkit's codes are not
documented and a template's code is unknown until a build emits it, so a row is matched by its tool, its code where it
names one, and its text (the template's fixed words, `{}` for a number or a name), and a row with no code matches the
template whatever code the toolkit gave it. A notice the ledger has no row for REFUSES the build and is quoted whole, with
the instruction to add a row (`notice_without_row`, which no declaration lifts); so nothing the toolkit says is dropped unread.

THE ONE ROW TYPE. A row is `tool, code, text, rule, allow, reason, fix`: the notice is counted under its RULE, at the function it names (a kernel's
notices of one rule are summed over its rows), and `allow` is the row's default allowance -- 0 when absent (a refusal: the experiment is not what it
measures, or the code is defective: a `setmaxnreg` the toolkit ignored, a variable read before it is set, a kernel that spills registers or keeps a stack frame;
the refusal prints the row's fix), a count, or `null` for a notice accepted by default (counted, printed in a pack's report where it may matter and its cost is not
measured -- C7519, the arrive the toolkit injected; #128-D, a loop proved unreachable: a measured loop that is not run measures nothing -- and silent for
the toolkit's per-function summary and a name nothing reads, #177-D). A DECLARATION beside the kernel overrides the row's allowance for the kernels its
pattern names, with its own reason, and may tighten as well as allow: an experiment declares the count of its hazard (`STEPS_SERIALIZED`, `WAIT_INJECTED`: the rules
`mma_serialized` and `wait_injected`, a kernel's notices of a hazard summed over its codes -- the form is sometimes the point), a production arm's
declarations (`docs/internals/tools/sass_gate.md#declarations`) do the same for the library. A row's reason names the `docs/lab/` page where it is a measured cost.

HOW A NEW NOTICE IS DECIDED. The refusal is the prompt: read what the notice says the toolkit did, and ask what it
does to the SASS the kernel runs. If the kernel it names no longer runs the stream its source states (a step
serialized, a wait or an arrive the source did not write, a register trade that did not happen, local memory traffic),
the row's allowance is 0, and a kernel that can mean the form declares its count (a new rule joins `HAZARD_RULES` under a hazard's name). If it
may matter and nothing measures it, the allowance is `null` and the notice is reported; the `docs/lab/` page that measures it is named in the row's reason. If it
cannot reach the SASS, `null` with the reason said, and the rule `build_info` or `unused_variable` where the pack's report is to leave it out. A new row lands in the same commit as the experiment that
printed it; a notice is never accepted for want of a decision, and acceptance of a notice whose cost is merely
unmeasured is a report.

THE ASSEMBLER'S OWN WARNINGS. The experiment's compile (`bazel/lab.bzl`'s `_COPTS`) passes ptxas `--warn-on-spills` (`-warn-spills`: "Warning if registers are
spilled to local memory"), `--warn-on-local-memory-usage` (`-warn-lmem-usage`: "Warning if local memory is used") and
`--verbose` (`-v`: "prints code generation statistics", the per-function summary) through nvcc's `-Xptxas`, so the
two warnings (`Registers are spilled to local memory in function 'k', N bytes spill stores, N bytes spill loads` and
`Local memory used for function 'k', size of stack frame: N bytes`) are ledger rows and refuse like any other. They are the lab's check of local memory: the frame's size is the warning's own.

<a id="waits"></a>THE WAIT REPORT. `pack` prints, and `bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- waits --arch sm_90a` prints alone,
one line a distinct tensor wait of each kernel (kernels with the same lines collapse, a count each; at most six lines a
kernel and sixteen groups of kernels): `wait(N): K candidates (loads a, stores b, async c, other d) before STOP` -- the instructions after the wait,
in straight-line code up to the next branch, barrier, tensor issue or wait (STOP), that touch none of the registers the
waited steps write nor read one a toucher wrote, by class. They are candidates to precede the wait; never a refusal.
NOT SEEN: past a branch (a loop's back-edge, a conditional), so a loop's next trip is not a candidate; the steps are the
`HGMMA`s since the previous wait in address order, not the control flow's group; a register is widened to the run its
operand width reaches (`.64`, `.WIDE`: two; `.128`: four), so no candidate touches an accumulator; memory is not traced
(a load after a store that read an accumulator is dropped, but a candidate store may still alias a load that stays after
the wait: the author checks the address dependence); a candidate is moved only if the arrival or barrier ordering it sits under allows.

<a id="steps"></a>THE STEP REPORTS. `pack` prints, and `bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- steps --arch sm_90a` prints alone, two lines a
kernel that holds tensor steps (kernels with the same lines collapse): what ONE step costs, read from the SASS, so a
prediction of a step's cost is made from these lines and not counted by hand. A kernel with several regions of steps
(each innermost loop that holds one, each step outside such a loop) prints their range and the figures of the one with
the most.
- `port: B a step (W wavefronts): operands o + shared s` is the bytes the step puts through the shared-memory port: the
  step's own operands, which its shape and its operand list give (a register operand is none), and the shared loads and
  stores of its region. A shared access between two steps is executed by each of the warpgroup's four warps, so `s` is
  four warps' (`x 4 warps`) while `o` is the warpgroup's, once. Wavefronts are the bytes over 128.
- `work: n instructions a step (class k, ...)` is the instructions of the region a thread executes for one step, in
  the classes a budget is stated in (`tools/sass.py`'s `OPCODE_CLASSES`, KERNEL_STANDARDS 29.1).
- A line that says a step is not read is a listing this does not parse: a defect of the report, never a pass.
  The empty group ptxas issues to hold a wait (`HGMMA ... RZ, gdesc[URZ], RZ`) is no step.

`Bench::expect(kernel, "STEP_PORT", "<=", bytes)` and `"STEP_WORK"` (the total instructions) are declared bounds, `<=`
only: undeclared, nothing is refused; declared, a region of the kernel over it refuses the run, and a bound that matches
no kernel with a step is refused too. The registers a kernel holds are not a bound here: ptxas's allocation is in the
pack file and a kernel that does not fit its launch bounds spills and is refused for local memory; nothing holds a
lab kernel to a role's register budget. `carry_token` bounds both by
the most of its rows and `wgmma_width` bounds `STEP_PORT` at 2,048 + 32 N.
NOT COUNTED: the copy engine's writes (bulk copies, the tensor memory access), bank conflicts, lanes masked off, generic
loads and stores that reach shared memory, atomics, a shared access outside the region, and trip counts: a loop's
figures are its mean over its steps, and both sides of a branch count once.

<a id="graph"></a>THE EXPERIMENTS ARE THE GRAPH'S TARGETS (`measure/lab/BUILD.bazel`, `bazel/lab.bzl`; Blake's ruling, 2026-11-21: no tool runs nvcc, cuobjdump or
nvdisasm for the lab outside the graph's actions). Each `measure/lab/experiments/<name>.cu` is the target `//measure/lab:<name>`: the graph's CUDA rule
(`cuda_unit`, the rule that builds the library) compiles it for sm_90a with the lab's flags and `cc_binary` links it as `<name>.sm_90a`. The target's outputs are
the binary and, beside it, the facts about its code that every reader of the experiment reads once: the toolkit's own listings and graphs of the binary, the
functions' names, the compile's diagnostics and the toolkit's version (`_FACTS` in `bazel/lab.bzl`, `PARTS` in `tools/lab.py`, written by
`tools/lab_facts.py`). THE CHECKS are the target's validation (`tools/lab_check.py`: the SASS checker, the notices' ledger and the experiment's declarations,
over those files, in the lab's own words), so `bazel build //measure/lab:<name>` refuses an experiment `run` would refuse, and so does its run target
(`<name>.run`), whose build runs the validation; `run`, `ncu` and `pack` check again what they read. A command that reports findings on an experiment the checks
refuse runs with them off (`bazel run --norun_validations //measure/lab:<name>.run -- sass`) and prints them itself; every command reads the files
(`lab.Built`) the run target hands it (`--built`, the directory of the build in its runfiles), and none runs a compiler, a disassembler or a build.
A pack holds the same files, so `ledger --packed` reads a packed experiment as the target is read. The lab builds sm_90a alone (the tree's one
architecture; a card of another is refused by name).

<a id="pack"></a>A CARD ELSEWHERE. A box that is not started from the devbox image ([`setup.md`](../../setup.md)) carries a driver and
no toolkit, so an experiment for it is built and checked here and run there: `bazel run //measure/lab:<name>.run -- pack --arch sm_90a --into DIR` builds the experiment's
target with its checks (a refusal fails the build, with the text `run` prints) and copies its outputs -- no compile of its own: an unchanged experiment is
Bazel's cache hit -- the binary, the files beside it that every reader of the experiment's code reads (the cubin, the listings,
the demangled names, the assembler's log, the toolkit's version: [below](#graph)) and a pack file (the kernels' registers
and opcodes, the toolkit, the commit, the binary's digest); on the card, `tools/lab.py carried <name> --from DIR --into
OUT [--no-counters HOST]` (the counters read where the box holds the pinned profiler, the host named where it grants
none) runs the pack's binary by its digest through the sequence `run` runs (`measure`: the inputs, a
quiet GPU, the repetitions under a kill timeout, `--timeout`, the checks; `--blocks N`, for `run` too, the grid held to N CTAs,
a lighter load on the card, a record of its own) and writes the record with the pack's build facts as files under `OUT/<name>/`
(`<arch>[.blocks<N>].json` and its `.trace/`), which the box hands back. `--no-counters HOST` is the caller's DECLARATION that the named host grants no profiler counters: the record
then carries the written exception in their place (`counters_exception`: the host, what stands in --
`docs/KERNEL_STANDARDS.md` §16 addendum 4). `run` takes the same flag; without it a profiler that fails fails the
run. An experiment with budgeted kernels, whose gate reads executed counts, is neither packed nor recorded under
the exception.

An experiment that reads data -- a cell's routing, say -- has a `<name>.inputs.py` beside it whose `write(directory)`
writes the files and returns their names. `run` and `ncu` call it first (in the scratch space), pass the binary
`--inputs <directory>`, and the record carries each file's digest under `inputs`.

## The standard counters

`run` reads the standard counter set of [`pipe_counters.py`](pipe_counters.md) for every kernel of the experiment (its
first launch) and prints, a unit (its MMAs, else its declared `Bench::units`, else a launch an SM), the profiler's own
busy share of the issue, integer and FMA pipes, L2 sectors and the three largest stall reasons; the record keeps the
counters under `counters`. It prices nothing: a count times a rate bounds perfect overlap and full use
(KERNEL_STANDARDS §28, §29 item 14), and what a composed kernel reaches is read from its timeline (KERNEL_STANDARDS `#stepped`).

<a id="warpline"></a>THE STAMP HEADER AND THE RECORD. `measure/lab/lab.cuh` includes `warpline/stamp.cuh` (the in-kernel stamp of the warpline module, pinned in
`MODULE.bazel`) and `warpline/host.hpp` (its host writer of the native record, plain C++17). the experiments' compile takes them as the dependencies `@warpline//:stamp` and `@warpline//:host` (`bazel/lab.bzl`). The includes are compile-time
only, so `pack` and `carried` need nothing of warpline at run time: the packed binary writes the records itself. Both entries
hand `tools/lab.py` the directory of warpline's Python package too (`FSLA_WARPLINE_PYTHON`, `@warpline//:warpline`), which
`tools/lab_trace.py` reads the records with and `lab.py` checks them with (`tools/checked.py` is the one seam to the checker); a box that runs a pack outside Bazel has none, and
`carried` there writes the records unchecked (`lab trace` checks them where warpline is).
`trace_record` is the one place the lab's row meets warpline's: the lab's row head (the count of entries issued and the CTA's shared base, 32 words)
is not warpline's (`kHead` entries, the count in the first), so a row is given to `Record::add_row` by its entries and its issued count, and
the entries are the lab's own but in three places -- the sites `entry` and `exit` are the launch's edges (a phase-begin mark there is
`kLaunchBegin` / `kLaunchEnd`), a barrier's site is read against the row's base, and the barriers' facts are inferred from the stamps (a site
named `... steps` is a warp's own steps, count 0; a copy's site is released by the copy, its one arrival not stamped, the copy its
issue, declared by `Record::operation`; a named barrier's arrival is not stamped, declared by `Record::rendezvous` with the skew
`kRendezvousSkew`, 128 cycles: a named barrier's warps are released by one event and each reads its clock after its
`bar.sync` returns, so one phase's wait ends differ by the read's lag -- in `carry_window`'s one-CTA record (its traced rows) by at most 87 cycles,
median 11, each at least 50 after the phase's last begin; 128 is the next power of two over that, not yet read from a
full grid's record; any other's count is the warps that arrive at it), and the variant by `Record::variant`.

## What goes wrong, and what guards it

Every item below cost a wrong number before the lab existed; each is now a guard or a habit.

* <a id="build"></a>**A launch sized at the host pass's card.** The host pass compiles at the pinned card, so a
  harness that sized its launch from a plan at the compiling architecture launched the A100 with sm_86's shared bytes
  (2026-11-07, `read_alone`, `read_dense`). An experiment compiles for one architecture (`bazel/lab.bzl`:
  the extension's own `-gencode` form for that one row, C++20 as the extension) and `-DFSLA_LAB_CC` names it to both passes, so `lab::kLabBuild` -- that
  architecture's own build ([`common/hardware.md#builds`](../common/hardware.md#builds)) -- is the one plan a harness
  reads on the device and on the host.
* **Dead work.** ptxas deletes an instruction whose result is dead — including a result only tested against a value it
  can fold (a probe's MMAs vanished behind `acc == x && m == y` with `m` constant). Guard: `lab::sink` stores
  unconditionally, and the expectations refuse a kernel missing what it means to time.
* **Rewritten arithmetic.** `asm volatile` binds the front end, not ptxas. ptxas folds a repeated `x += k`, rewrites a
  whole integer-add recurrence (adds are exactly associative), merges two-input adds into the three-input `IADD3`, and
  issues adds as `IMAD.IADD` on the FMA pipe at will. Habit: an instruction's operands are loop-variant values from
  other chains; integer adds are timed paired with a non-associative op; an expectation may name alternatives
  (`IADD3|IMAD.IADD`).
* **Collectives under role-dependent control flow.** A warp collective under a branch on a thread-derived value — even
  a warp-uniform role like `threadIdx.x >> 5`, and even when the role only sets a loop's trip count — compiles into a
  CALL into a convergence subroutine. Guard: the driver refuses an undeclared CALL; `collective_branch` records the
  behaviour.
* **A sink that cancels.** XOR-folding a value twice folds to zero, and ptxas deleted a whole kernel's MMAs behind
  it. Guard: the expectations.
* **The mean warp undercounts.** When warps finish apart, the mean warp's cycles read below the tensor floor; the
  block's span is the throughput. Habit: `WarpSpan` wherever warps can drift.
* **Light register pressure.** A step that runs at its floor beside 150 registers may spill beside the kernel's 255.
  Habit: an experiment about a kernel's step holds the kernel's live accumulators.
* **Runtime choices inside a timed loop.** A `kind` argument switched at run time inside the loop measured branches, not
  MMAs. Habit: every variant is a template parameter.
* **The operand pattern.** An instruction's cost beside the tensor pipe grows with the registers it reads: packed f16
  math with a constant operand measured ~4x cheaper than with two registers. Habit: write the operand pattern the
  kernels use.
* **Phase lock.** Two warps a scheduler can fall into a slow relative phase on some launches (up to 22% for a pure HMMA
  stream). Guard: repetitions; the driver flags spread.
* **A busy GPU.** Another consumer time-slices every kernel. Guard: the driver refuses above 5% utilization before a
  run (`--force` to override).
